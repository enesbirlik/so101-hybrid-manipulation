# Day 3 — Hand-eye calibration, and a 10° shoulder error it uncovered

**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Roadmap step:** 3. Calibration, URDF matching, fisheye camera calibration · **Status:** hand-eye done, elbow/wrist zeros open

This step measures where the wrist camera sits on the gripper (the "hand-eye" transform). Once that is known, anything the camera sees can be placed in the robot's base frame. The setup is the usual eye-in-hand one:
- the ChArUco board from [day 2](2026-10-28-day-2.md) lies fixed on the table,
- the arm views it from many poses,
- for each pose, the gripper pose comes from forward kinematics (FK) and the board pose comes from the camera.

Because the board never moves, the correct camera transform puts the board at the same place in `base_link` every time. How much that place spreads across samples is the quality measure used throughout this entry.

## What I did

- Wrote [`tools/so101_hand_eye.py`](../../tools/so101_hand_eye.py). FK is computed straight from the URDF and the joint calibration, so ROS does not need to run. The tool has three commands:
  - `capture`: move the arm by hand with torque off.
  - `auto`: torque on, the arm visits poses around a centre pose on its own.
  - `solve`: runs all five `cv2.calibrateHandEye` methods and keeps the most consistent one.
- `auto` keeps every pose safe:
  - inside the calibrated joint ranges,
  - at least 5 cm above the table,
  - with the board predicted to be in view,
  - away from the joint ranges where the [day-1](2026-10-27-day-1.md) jerk problem is predicted.

  Every pose is reached twice, once from above and once from below, so the effect of play can be measured directly.
- Captured 18 samples by hand and 3 `auto` runs (114 samples), each run around a different centre pose.

It runs from `gripper_link` to the OpenCV optical frame of the upright image. The camera's optical axis points mostly along −z of `gripper_link`: (0.02, −0.44, −0.90).

## What I learned

- **Never touch the arm while capturing.** Capturing by hand with torque off gave a 15 mm spread. Gravity and my hand pushed each joint around inside its gear play, and the joint can also wobble sideways, which the encoder cannot see. With torque on, each servo holds its output shaft and nobody touches the arm, and the spread dropped to 3 mm.
- **Measure play instead of guessing it.** Approaching the same pose from both sides, the servos stop about 2.2° apart. That is their deadband. The camera sees the same 2.0°, and only 0.14° (median) is missed by the encoders. So with torque on, play is not the limit.
- **A free nuisance parameter can hide an error.** I tried to estimate joint zero offsets together with the camera and board poses. With the board pose free, the `shoulder_lift` offset came out +4.9°, +9.6° and +6.1° in the three runs, far apart given their ±1° uncertainty, so I first rejected it. The free board pose was absorbing the error by tilting and lifting the board.
- **Use what you know about the scene.** The board lies on the table the arm stands on, and `base_link` sits at table level. Yet FK put the board **38 mm above the table and tilted by 9°**. Pinning the board to the table plane (z = 0, horizontal, only x, y and yaw free) made the three runs agree: **shoulder_lift +10.0°, +10.5°, +11.0°**. This is the camera version of "touch the table to calibrate". It also explains something I had noticed before: in RViz the model touched the table before the real arm did.
- After moving the `shoulder_lift` zero by +10.5°, the rotation spread halved (1.66° → 0.71°), the board tilt dropped from 9° to 2.2°, and the third run agrees with the first two again. Fitting the offset once more gives ≈ 0 in every run (−0.4°, +0.1°, +0.5°).

## Problems & fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 15 mm spread with hand-held capture | Joint play and wobble under gravity and hand force, not seen by the encoders | `auto`: torque on, no contact, each pose approached from both sides |
| Crash as soon as the board was seen | `cv2.fisheye.projectPoints` (OpenCV 4.6) rejects a strided matrix column | Pass a contiguous copy |
| Joint offsets disagree between runs | Free board pose absorbs the shoulder error | Pin the board to the table plane |
| Board 38 mm above the table, 9° tilted | `shoulder_lift` zero off by ~10.5° (it was set by eye) | Zero tick 2048 → 1929 in [`so101_calibration.yaml`](../../so101_bringup/config/so101_calibration.yaml). EEPROM unchanged. |

## Open

- **elbow_flex and wrist_flex:** with the table constraint, runs 1 and 3 agree (elbow ≈ −3°, wrist_flex ≈ +2°), but run 2, centred on a very different elbow angle, gives −6.2° and +4.4°. They are not applied. A configuration-dependent error, such as link lengths that differ from the URDF, fits better than a constant zero offset.
- **The board still sits 13 mm below the table and is tilted 2.2°.** Together with the 4.7 mm spread, that is the current FK accuracy of the arm in this workspace.
- **Re-running `so101_calibrate.py calibrate` on `shoulder_lift` would undo the fix**, because it sets the zero from a hand-held pose again. The predicted jerk zone for `shoulder_lift` moved with the zero (−98° → −87.5°, the same encoder position).
