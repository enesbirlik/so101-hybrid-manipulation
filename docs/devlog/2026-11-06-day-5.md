# Day 5 — ros2_control and MoveIt 2 on the real arm

**Date:** 2026-11-01 (evening) and 2026-11-06 · **Roadmap step:** 4. C++ `ros2_control` interface + MoveIt 2 · **Status:** in progress (the arm plans and moves through MoveIt)

Until now every tool opened the serial bus itself. This step puts the arm behind `ros2_control`, so MoveIt plans and the controllers execute. A C++ hardware interface becomes the only owner of the bus.

## What I did

- **[`so101_description`](../../so101_description/):**
  - `ros2_control:=true` adds position command and position/velocity state for the six joints, with mock hardware or the real bus.
  - `table:=true` adds the table as a link at the measured `table_z` (day 4), so planning keeps the hand out of it.
- **[`so101_bringup`](../../so101_bringup/):**
  - The control launch: `controller_manager` at 100 Hz, a joint state broadcaster, and two trajectory controllers, one for the 5-joint arm and one for the gripper.
  - [`so101_ready.py`](../../so101_bringup/scripts/so101_ready.py) takes the arm out of its folded rest, which MoveIt refuses as a start state.
- **[`so101_moveit_config`](../../so101_moveit_config/)**, written by hand:
  - SRDF with groups and named poses.
  - [pick_ik](https://github.com/PickNikRobotics/pick_ik); OMPL and Pilz planners.
  - Joint limits cut to the calibrated ranges.
  - A demo launch with the wrist camera.
- **[`so101_hardware`](../../so101_hardware/)**, a C++ `SystemInterface`:
  - Per 10 ms cycle: one SYNC READ of all positions and speeds, one SYNC WRITE of all goals.
  - Ticks ↔ radians from the joint calibration.
  - An exclusive lock on the port, a homing-offset check at startup, and hold-in-place on activation.
- **[`so101_perception`](../../so101_perception/):**
  - A wrist camera publisher: upright image, fisheye `camera_info`, and a JPEG `compressed` topic.
  - The hand-eye transform in TF, and the field of view as an RViz marker.
- **Tests:** everything was tested on mock hardware first. Checks that must not disturb a running session use a separate `ROS_DOMAIN_ID`.

## Results

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Mock: joint goals (zero, rest, gripper) | executed within 0.01 rad |
| IK on dragged-marker-like targets (reachable position, orientation 17° off) | 0/30 with an orientation weight → 29/30 position-only, 0.1 mm, 5 ms |
| Real arm: control loop | `/joint_states` at 98 Hz, no bus errors |
| Real arm: fast pan step (25° in 0.6 s) | 95 % reached at 0.72 s, max tracking error 4.9° |
| Camera node CPU while viewed | 11–31 % → 5–7 % of one core |
| Compressed image rate | a steady 15 Hz (raw 690 kB frames: ~11 Hz arrive) |

## What I learned

- **A 5-joint arm cannot reach most 6-D poses.**
  - With an orientation weight, IK failed on nearly every pose the RViz marker was dragged to, and the marker snapped back.
  - Position-only IK follows the marker, and the gripper orientation then follows from the position.
  - A top-down grasp needs its own solver: position plus "jaws pointing down", which a 5-joint arm can reach.
- **The arm crept and shook when it moved slowly.**
  - At 2 rad/s and 4 rad/s² limits with 0.1–0.3 scaling, each 10 ms command moved less than the servos' deadband. The servos stuck, then jumped, and a move took 8–12 s.
  - Measured directly, the servos follow fast commands well.
  - Raising the limits to 3 rad/s and 8 rad/s² and planning at 50–100 % fixed it.
- **The servo gains are a trade-off.** The servos' position-loop gain (P) is 32 from the factory; I lowered it to 16, LeRobot's value for the SO-101.
  - Writing the gains with the EEPROM lock on only changes RAM: no EEPROM wear, and the factory values return after a power cycle.
  - With P 16, the arm stops 2–3° short under its own weight.
  - Replaying a hand-taught motion therefore runs with P 32.
- **The folded rest is not a valid start state.**
  - `shoulder_lift` lies past its planning limit, the forearm touches the shoulder in the model, and the hand often rests on the table.
  - An explicit elbow-first unfold, before handing over to MoveIt, is simpler than loosening the collision model.
- **The webcam has its own surprise.** `CAP_PROP_BUFFERSIZE 1`, which is meant to cut latency, halved the camera's frame rate. A capture thread that takes every frame does the same job without that cost.

## Problems & fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `ros2_control_node`: no `ros2_control` tag | An older RViz session still published a `/robot_description` without it | Stop old sessions, or use a separate `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` |
| Controllers had no `type` | Jazzy wants the types under `controller_manager` | Moved them there |
| `START_STATE_IN_COLLISION` (forearm/shoulder, hand/table) | The folded rest | `so101_ready.py` |
| Execution rejected: start deviates > 0.05 rad | The arm settled under load between plan and execute | `allowed_start_tolerance` 0.1 rad |
| The dragged marker did not move | Orientation-weighted IK on a 5-joint arm | Position-only pick_ik |
| Moves crept and shook | Commands below the servo deadband per cycle | Faster limits and scaling |
| Camera at 11–13 Hz instead of 15 | A one-frame buffer, a publish timer that rounded gaps up, and large raw messages | Capture thread, publishing on a schedule, compressed topic |
