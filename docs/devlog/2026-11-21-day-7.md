# Day 7 — Gains that didn't help, a gripper that couldn't close, and a first grasp attempt

**Date:** 2026-11-21 · **Roadmap step:** 4. C++ `ros2_control` interface + MoveIt 2 (model accuracy on the real arm) · **Status:** in progress

The plan was to finish the open items of [day 6](2026-11-14-day-6.md): per-joint servo gains for the pan shake, a fresh check of the table model, and the gripper. At the end of the day I tried the first real task: find a toy (a Woody figure) on the table with the wrist camera and pick it up. It got as far as hovering over the toy. Then the wrist camera dropped off the USB bus.

## What I did

**Per-joint gains** (commit `355137e`).
- `so101_hardware` now reads `p_coefficient`, `d_coefficient` and `i_coefficient` for each joint from the `ros2_control` block. A joint value wins over a hardware-level value, and that wins over the built-in 16 / 32 / 0.
- In the xacro they are set through one `joint_gains` table, for example `dict(shoulder_pan=dict(p=32))`.
- Unit tests cover the defaults, the override order, invalid values, and parsing from a real URDF.

**The pan sweep, repeated per setting.** I moved `shoulder_pan` ±17° at a constant speed with the arm stretched out, and measured from the controller's reference and feedback.

| `shoulder_pan` P/D/I | Lag at 0.05 rad/s | Ripple p-p at 0.05 | Stalls > 80 ms | Lag at 0.3 rad/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **16/32/0 (default)** | 0.62° | 0.56° | 55 | 1.76° |
| 24/32/0 | 0.44° | 0.68° | 38 (now also at 0.1 rad/s) | 1.32° |
| 32/32/0 | 0.35° | 0.85° | 9 | 1.09° |
| 24/64/0 | 0.43° | 0.67° | 28 | 1.32° |
| 16/64/0 | 0.58° | 0.58° | 47 | 1.75° |
| 16/32/4 | 0.09° | 0.81° | 45 (up to 230 ms) | 1.93° |

- **No setting removes the stick-slip.** Higher P cuts the lag but makes the 4–5 Hz ripple bigger, and it starts at higher speeds too. D changes almost nothing. I removes the average lag at low speed, but the stalls get longer, so each jump gets harder.
- The defaults stay at 16/32/0. The fix for the pan is on the motion side: don't drive it very slowly or in tiny steps.

**The ruler check, repeated through ROS.** This time the arm was driven through `arm_controller`, not by a standalone tool. The script works out the elbow angle that puts the lowest point of the hand 15 mm above the table, approaches from 40 mm higher, and holds there. Then I measure the gap with a ruler.

| Reach | Model | Ruler | Ruler − model |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 cm | 11.9 | 13.5 | +1.6 |
| 25 cm | 11.2 | 13.5 | +2.3 |
| 28 cm | 5.5 | 9 | +3.5 |
| 31 cm | 12.1 | 13 | +0.9 |
| 34 cm | 10.2 | 10 | −0.2 |
| 34 cm, wrist 45° | 10.3 | 9 | −1.3 |
| 38 cm | 6.2 | 4 | −2.2 |
| 25 cm, pan 25° | 5.4 | 6 | +0.6 |

- 1.9 mm rms, worst 3.5 mm (day 6: 2.6 mm rms). The day-6 correction holds.
- The same pattern as day 6 is still there: the model is 2–3 mm low near the base and about 2 mm high at full reach.
- The bigger error is elsewhere. `shoulder_lift` stopped 1–2° away from its goal, so the hand hovered 5–10 mm away from the planned height. The model follows the encoders and is still right. But the arm is not where it was sent.

**The gripper could not close.**
- Commanded to −10° (the closed stop), it stopped at −7.5° and stayed there. The load was only 3.6 %, and the wrist camera showed a gap between the jaw tips.
- P 32 on the gripper alone changed nothing, so it was not a weak loop.
- A `scan` gave the answer. The servo's EEPROM minimum was 2067 ticks, but the measured closed end is 2044. The calibration tool writes every limit 2° inside the measured range, so that the servo never pushes into a hard stop. For the gripper, the closed end *is* where it has to push.
- Fix (commit `231fbce`): EEPROM minimum set to 2044 on this arm. The calibration tool keeps the gripper's closed end from now on. The SRDF `closed` pose went from −0.1 rad (−5.7°, still ~5 mm open) to −0.1745 rad (−10°), so the gripper squeezes.
- Now it closes to −9.05°, and the load at the stop rises to 5.6 %.

**Jaw opening versus angle.**
- I measured the gap between the jaw tips with a ruler: 13 mm at 0°, 40 mm at 20°. That gives about **13 mm + 1.35 mm per degree**, up to ~30°.
- The URDF meshes give the same slope but read about 2.8 mm too wide.
- On a slow close (0.5° steps), the jaws meet at about −7.5° to −9°. There the gap between command and measured angle grows from 0.3° to 0.95°, the load goes from 2.8 to 5.6 % and the current doubles. At P 16 that gap is a clearer contact signal than the load.

**A first grasp attempt.**
- *Finding the toy.* I put the camera in a high pose looking down, then turned the pan in steps (±25°, ±45°). Woody showed up lying near the edge of the table.
- *Locating it.* I took the pixels of the boots, the torso and the hat, undistorted them with the fisheye model, and intersected the rays with a plane 2 cm above the table, using the hand-eye pose. That put the toy at 30–45 cm reach, about 16 cm long, with the torso at (36, 8) cm.
- *The approach.* At that reach the jaws cannot point straight down: `wrist_flex` runs into its 95° limit. A small IK asked for the tip at the torso, the jaws closing across the body axis, and the approach as close to vertical as possible. It found a solution with the jaws tilted 10°.
- *Hovering.* The arm went to 7 cm above the target. Seen from the side, the hand was **above and slightly beyond** the toy, not above the torso. The hand-eye pose was fitted with the old shoulder zero (day 6), so this is the expected kind of error.
- *The camera dropped.* Reaching that pose needed `wrist_roll` at −124°. At that moment the camera disconnected from USB, and it did not come back on any port. The USB cable is soldered straight onto the camera module's pads (there was no matching connector), and the **VCC wire tore off its pad**.
- I took the arm back up, then to zero, then to the folded rest, and switched the torque off.

## What I learned

- **Gains cannot fix stick-slip on this servo.** The P/D/I table above shows trade-offs, not a fix. The useful lever is the motion profile.
- **Safety margins can be wrong for one joint.** A 2° margin protects the arm joints from their hard stops. For a gripper it means the jaws never meet.
- **Look before you tune.** The gripper problem looked like weak gains, and P 32 did not change it. A single register read found the real cause.
- **The tracking error, not the model, now limits accuracy.** The ruler check agrees with the model to 2 mm, while the arm itself lands 5–10 mm off its goal.
- **The wrist roll needs a limit, and the cable needs strain relief.** The jaws are symmetric, so +56° gives the same grasp as −124°. Roll should stay within ±90°. A soldered cable needs a fixing point right behind the board, so that a pull ends there and not on the solder pad.

## Problems & fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pan shakes at low speed | Stick-slip; no P/D/I setting removes it | Defaults kept; avoid very slow pan moves |
| Gripper stops at −7.5°, jaw tips ~2 mm apart | Calibration wrote the 2° limit margin on the closed end too | EEPROM min 2067 → 2044; tool keeps the gripper's closed end; SRDF `closed` −10° |
| The SRDF `closed` pose left the jaws ~5 mm open | −0.1 rad was a guess | −0.1745 rad |
| The grasp pose ended above and beyond the toy | Hand-eye was fitted with the old shoulder zero | **Open:** refit hand-eye |
| Wrist camera off the USB bus | `wrist_roll` −124° pulled the soldered cable; VCC wire torn off its pad | **Open:** re-solder, add strain relief, limit roll to ±90° |
| The per-joint gain unit test failed | The test URDF had no `<joint>` elements; the parser requires them | Test URDF fixed |
