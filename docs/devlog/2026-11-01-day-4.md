# Day 4 — Kinematic calibration, and the play that fooled every contact test

**Date:** 2026-10-29 and 2026-11-01 · **Roadmap step:** 3. Calibration, URDF matching, fisheye camera calibration · **Status:** done

[Day 3](2026-10-28-day-3.md) ended with a corrected `shoulder_lift` zero and two open questions:
- Were the `elbow_flex` and `wrist_flex` zeros right?
- Why did the board still sit 13 mm below the table?

This entry follows those questions through three methods:
1. **Touching the table:** did not work.
2. **A full kinematic calibration from the camera:** worked.
3. **A ruler and a screwdriver:** found what the first method had been tripping over.

## What I did

**2026-10-29: the day-1 jerk.** Day 1 left an open problem. With torque on, `shoulder_lift` and `elbow_flex` jerked into their stops. The suspect was the raw encoder wrapping (4095 → 0) inside a joint range.
- [`tools/so101_wrap_test.py`](../../tools/so101_wrap_test.py) sweeps one joint slowly across its wrap point at low torque.
  - `shoulder_pan` crossed it both ways, as did `elbow_flex`. Max lag was 1.6° and current ≤ 110 mA.
  - The wrap theory is rejected.
- The stall seen with `zero` had a simpler cause. Lifting the shoulder first with the elbow folded swung the jaw into the table. `zero` now unfolds the elbow first.

**Table touch.** The idea: lower the hand onto the table until the joint stops following its goal. At each touch, the lowest point of the hand meshes must lie on the table. That constrains the joint zeros without a camera.
- [`tools/so101_table_touch.py`](../../tools/so101_table_touch.py) plans the poses, lowers the hand with `elbow_flex`, and fits the offsets.
- On synthetic touches it recovers known offsets within 0.2°.
- On the real arm it failed:
  - At every touch, the model put the hand 20–47 mm *below* the table.
  - The fit asked for a −14 to −17° change of `shoulder_lift`.
  - After the hand reached the table, the elbow kept turning about 20°.
- A gentler check stops 15 mm above the table and asks for the gap seen. It matched the model within about 5 mm, so the joints were not that far off.

**More camera data.** The hand-eye `auto` mode got three changes:
- `--spread` widens the pose spread, so the shoulder and elbow vary apart.
- `--side DEG` computes a new centre pose looking at the board from the side.
- A mask for the jaws, which cover the bottom of every image.

The camera brushed the arm twice in one side run. Its data was 21–27 px off with any model and is excluded.

**Kinematic calibration.** [`so101_hand_eye.py kinematics`](../../tools/so101_hand_eye.py) fits a set of parameters together on the corner reprojection error.

Fitted:
- the camera pose;
- one free board pose per board placement;
- the `wrist_flex` origin, which moves in the plane perpendicular to its axis;
- small tilts of the `shoulder_lift`, `elbow_flex` and `wrist_flex` axes.

Left out:
- zero offsets: with the geometry free, they came out within 1°;
- an `elbow_flex` shift: it did not lower the held-out error.

How the fit is checked:
- **Leave one run out:** fit without one run, then test on that run.
- **New board placement:** fit on the old placements, then test on a new one.

The result goes to [`so101_description/config/so101_kinematics.yaml`](../../so101_description/config/so101_kinematics.yaml). The URDF reads it through xacro, and the Python tools read the same file, so RViz, MoveIt and the tools all use the same model. The file keeps the upstream values as `cad`.

**Finding the play.** In RViz, with the hand resting on the table (torque off), the jaw went 15 mm through the floor. Three measurements found out why:
- **Resting poses:** 12 poses, each with the hand resting on the table and torque off. The model put the hand 10–37 mm below the table. A `shoulder_lift` change of −6° alone would explain them, which contradicted every torque-on measurement.
- **Replaying the rests:** each resting pose revisited with torque on, with `shoulder_lift` 6° higher. The gap under the hand was measured by putting a finger in and measuring that height with a ruler. The gap was 10–36 mm smaller than the encoders said.
- **Pushing the hand at zero:** the hand lifted 2–3 cm while the encoders saw about 1 cm. Most of the motion was at `wrist_flex`.

Tightening the `wrist_flex` screws took the camera fit error over all samples from 6.4 px to 2.7 px. I refitted on two fresh runs.

## Results

| Measure | CAD geometry | Calibrated |
|---|---|---|
| Held-out runs, before tightening (7 runs, 3 board placements) | 10.6 px | 7.6 px |
| New board placement, fitted on the old ones only | 16.5 px | 9.2 px |
| Held-out runs, after tightening (2 runs) | 9.9 px | 4.8 px |
| Ruler gaps under the hand, torque on (6 poses) | — | 1.1 mm RMS |

How to read the table:
- The first two rows use the geometry fitted before the wrist screws were tightened. The last two use the final one.
- At ~25 cm, 1 px is about 0.5 mm.
- The ruler row uses the measured table height (`table_z`, see below).

Final geometry:
- `wrist_flex` sits 11 mm farther along the forearm than in CAD.
- The axes tilt by up to 1.4°.

The joint zeros and the EEPROM are unchanged.

## What I learned

- **Play depends on the load, and contact reverses the load.**
  - When the arm carries its own weight, the play in the wrist joint takes up one way, the same way at every pose. The model error was then constant (±1.3 mm).
  - When the hand rests on the table or pushes against it, the play takes up the other way. That shifts the hand by 1–3 cm, unseen by the encoders.
  - This is why the touch tests failed and why the resting poses asked for −6°. For an arm that will work under torque, calibrate it under torque, in free space.
- **Fix the mechanics before modelling them.** A few turns of a screwdriver halved the camera error. That is more than every model parameter together.
- **A fitted geometry is an effective model, not a measurement.** In RViz, the wrist meshes now overlap the forearm, and the real parts do not.
  - The 11 mm stands for whatever moves the wrist as the camera sees it: assembly offsets, horn mounting, leftover play.
  - It earns its place only through the held-out tests.
- **Some parameters are invisible to the data.**
  - The camera cannot see the table height when the board pose is free. A pure vertical shift of the arm moves the board with it.
  - Left free, the `shoulder_lift` tilt about the pan axis does the same job as a pan offset. It drifted to 5° and moved the modelled gripper 22 mm sideways, with no effect on the residuals. It is now held at zero.
- **Test on data the fit has never seen.** Several models fitted better on their own data. Only leaving out whole runs or whole board placements showed which of them predicted better.

## Problems & fixes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `zero` stalled from the folded rest pose | Lifting first swung the jaw into the table | Unfold the elbow first (stage order) |
| `zero` stalled from a pose leaning on the table | The elbow-first order pushed the hand into the table there | Teleop `z` (lift first). **Open:** pick the order from the start pose |
| Table touch asked for −14 to −17° on `shoulder_lift` | Wrist play reverses under contact; the detector also fired late | Not used for calibration; kept as a hover check |
| The board was hidden in side poses | The jaws cover the bottom of every image | `JAW_POLYGON` mask, aim the board above the jaws |
| The camera brushed the arm at `--side 20` | No self-collision check in `auto` | Run excluded. **Open:** add a check |
| The modelled gripper sat 22 mm sideways at zero | A `shoulder_lift` tilt about the pan axis is a pan offset in disguise | That tilt direction is left out of the fit |
| The hand went 1–3 cm further than the encoders said | Loose `wrist_flex` screws | Tightened; then refit on fresh data |
| The camera put the table 12.5 mm too high | Free board pose; the table height is invisible | `table_z` from ruler gaps, torque on |

## Open

- **Some play remains.**
  - Torque off with the hand on the table still differs from torque on by 5–28 mm. In free space under torque, this does not matter.
  - For contact (grasping, pushing), it will. Contact detection should wait for current or load sensing in `ros2_control`.
- **`table_z` belongs to the current geometry.** Measure it again after every refit.
- **Pinning the board to the table** makes the camera fit worse (7.1 px vs 2.7 px). The board is either not flat, or its height is not what I assumed.
